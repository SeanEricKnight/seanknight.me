About me

The same job, under a surprising number of titles.

I’ve spent most of my career where technology, customers, operations, security, and leadership collide.

I started in hands-on technical work, built and ran small businesses, led technical teams, managed enterprise healthcare implementations and strategic accounts, and eventually moved into information security governance.

The titles changed. The work underneath them really didn’t: find the actual problem, translate between people who are using the same words to mean different things, and keep responsibility visible until something useful happens.

I like systems, history, good questions, useful tools, people who can say “I don’t know,” and ideas that survive being poked with a stick.

I live in Spring Hill, Tennessee, near Nashville.

What I do

Technology is usually the easy part.

The harder part is deciding what problem matters, what risk is real, what tradeoff is acceptable, and whether everyone in the room is actually talking about the same thing.

Ministry

Christianity, apparently.

Faith without pretending the questions aren’t there.

I make very short videos about Christianity, Scripture, doubt, grace, and questions that deserve more than a bumper-sticker answer.

The project is called Christianity, apparently. The point isn’t to manufacture certainty or win theological arguments. It’s to notice what the text actually says, ask the uncomfortable question, and keep looking.

Some of the videos are only a few seconds long. That is partly a creative constraint and partly because the internet has done enough talking already.
