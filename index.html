What I’m building now

Christianity, apparently.

I started Christianity, apparently. because I wanted a place to ask questions about Christianity that often get answered too quickly.

I’m a Christian, but doubt and unanswered questions don’t strike me as enemies of faith. I’d rather look directly at what Scripture says, including the awkward bits, and keep going.

The project started as very short YouTube videos — sometimes only a few seconds — built around a question or detail worth noticing. I’m building the site alongside the channel so those short prompts have somewhere to lead when the question needs more than ten seconds.

The aim is modest: help somebody think, reconsider, or hang on long enough to keep looking.

AI Lab

Things I’m trying to understand about local AI.

I’m building AI Lab as an engineering laboratory: part software project, part apprenticeship, part attempt to make sure I understand the thing before I automate it.

The lab has two equal outputs: reliable software and better engineering judgment. Code records what I built; the lab also preserves why I built it, how decisions were made, what failed, and what I learned.

The local LLMs run on a PowerSpec G913 with a Ryzen 9 9900X3D and GeForce RTX 5080. JARVIS, my Unraid server, does most of the storage, Linux testing, Docker work, and other infrastructure experiments. There are, inevitably, additional duties.

The current roadmap moves from foundation to digital twin, knowledge graph, automation, and interfaces. The working loop is: Idea → Hypothesis → Experiment → Build → Verify → Knowledge Check → Reflection → Lab Report → Commit.

Build. Verify. Learn. Repeat.
