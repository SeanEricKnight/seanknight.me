What I’m building now

Christianity, apparently.

I started Christianity, apparently. because I wanted a place to ask the kinds of questions about Christianity that often get answered too quickly.

I’m a Christian. I also have doubts, objections, unanswered questions, and a persistent suspicion that faith becomes less interesting when we pretend those things aren’t there.

The project began with very short videos — sometimes only a few seconds long — built around one question, one strange detail in Scripture, or one idea worth carrying around for a while.

Not sermons. Not apologetics disguised as certainty. Not another person on the internet explaining everything.

Mostly questions.

Why was Jesus amazed? What if God actually loves you? How did Christianity somehow lose track of what happened to Paul?

The point isn’t to win arguments. It’s to make room for thought, doubt, curiosity, grace, and maybe enough faith to keep looking.

I’m still figuring out what the project becomes. That is, conveniently, part of the project.

About

A short version.

I’ve spent most of my career working with technology and the people attempting to operate it.

I’ve built businesses, led technical teams, managed large healthcare implementations and strategic accounts, and worked in information security governance and risk.

The titles changed. The underlying work usually didn’t: find the actual problem, notice when people are using the same words to mean different things, and keep responsibility visible until something useful happens.

I’m a Christian, though not one who finds doubt particularly alarming. I’d rather ask an uncomfortable question than protect a comfortable answer.

I live in Spring Hill, Tennessee, with my wife, Erica.

I tend to like
old books, dark paintings, history, grace, weird systems, good arguments, useful tools, and people who can say “I don’t know.”
I tend to distrust
unnecessary certainty, managerial theater, polished religiosity, euphemisms, and any process explained primarily by tradition.

The interesting part usually starts one question after the obvious one.
